# Three-Minute Video Script

**Title:** *VoltRelay: From Growth to Profitable Growth*  
**Audience:** VoltRelay operations and customer-experience leadership  
**Tone:** calm, evidence-first, operator-to-operator  
**Runtime:** 3:00 (≈450 words)

---

### 0:00–0:20 — Hook

**Visual:** The growth chart (completed swaps and failures on the same timeline). The failure line climbs faster than the swap line.

**Narration:**  
"VoltRelay nearly tripled its swaps in eighteen months. It also made six times as many failures. Both of those are true at the same time — and that is the whole problem. Growth is real. The question is whether it is healthy."

**On-screen text:**  
`Swaps ×2.9 · Failures ×6.0 · Failure rate 4.4% → 8.6%`

---

### 0:20–0:50 — Approach

**Visual:** Dataset map: eight tables, 3.9M swap attempts, 1.5M station-hours, 6,500 batteries, 44,000 tickets.

**Narration:**  
"We rebuilt the unit economics from the event level. Every completed swap carries revenue, energy cost, battery wear, and its share of station fixed cost. Then we joined each attempt to the station's hourly telemetry and to city weather. That let us separate correlation from mechanism: where failures happen, when they happen, and what they cost."

**On-screen text:**  
`3.8M attempts · full contribution margin · station-hour telemetry · weather`

---

### 0:50–1:10 — Insight 1: heat

**Visual:** The cabinet-temperature failure curve breaking at 36–38°C; then the Gen1 charge-time and inventory panel.

**Narration:**  
"The first finding is physical. Failure is flat near four percent — until the cabinet crosses thirty-six to thirty-eight degrees. Above that, it doubles. At forty-five degrees, Gen1 cabinets take a hundred and fifty-six minutes to charge a pack, versus eighty-eight in cool hours. Charged inventory falls from almost nine packs to three. Gen3 barely moves. That single mechanism explains about thirty-nine thousand failures — seventeen percent of the total."

**On-screen text:**  
`36°C threshold · Gen1: 156 min, 3.1 packs · 39,445 heat-attributable failures`

---

### 1:10–1:30 — Insight 2: battery lot

**Visual:** Kyron cohort bars at 60.5% SOH versus 80% peers; wear per swap and low-range tickets.

**Narration:**  
"The second finding is asset quality, and it is specific. Kyron lots KY-2407, 08 and 09 retired at sixty point five percent state of health — all of them — after four hundred swaps per pack instead of six hundred and forty. Their wear cost is three times the peer average. Riders feel it as range: sixty-five kilometres at ninety percent health, forty-three at sixty to seventy. The avoidable wear is roughly twelve point seven crore rupees."

**On-screen text:**  
`60.5% SOH · ₹30.79 vs ₹9.56 wear/swap · ₹12.7M avoidable`

---

### 1:30–1:50 — Insight 3: partners and churn

**Visual:** Partner bubble chart (ZipDrop at far right, lowest margin); then the retention-by-exposure bars.

**Narration:**  
"Third: the largest partner is the weakest economically. ZipDrop is more than half a million swaps, but earns six rupees of contribution per swap against a sixteen-rupee network average. And churn is downstream of the same service problem. Among new riders still active in week four, repeated early failures cut ninety-day retention — the odds of returning fall by about a third for every ten-point increase in failure exposure. A bad first attempt alone does not matter. Repeated failure does."

**On-screen text:**  
`ZipDrop: ₹6.17 vs ₹16.53 CM/swap · failure exposure OR 0.64`

---

### 1:50–2:35 — Recommendation

**Visual:** Prioritised action table / three-step roadmap.

**Narration:**  
"So the budget question answers itself. Before adding stations, fix what is already in the field. First, retrofit or upgrade Gen1 cabinets for heat: it is the root cause of seventeen percent of failures and a direct retention lever. Second, quarantine the Kyron lots and write SOH and range acceptance into the next battery contract. Third, reopen ZipDrop's terms — or cap its growth — instead of scaling the least profitable volume in the network. Then extend peak pricing beyond Bengaluru and Pune, and treat queue abandonment as a top-tier incident. Add stations last, targeted at hot, high-abandonment cells with Gen3 equipment."

**On-screen text:**  
`1. Heat retrofit · 2. Battery acceptance · 3. Partner terms · 4. Peak pricing · 5. Add stations last`

---

### 2:35–3:00 — Close

**Visual:** Return to the title card; three numbers: `₹20.4M value at stake · 17.3% of failures heat-attributable · 5.6pp retention gap`.

**Narration:**  
"VoltRelay does not have a demand problem. It has a physics problem, a procurement problem, and a contract problem. Fix those three, and growth becomes profitable growth — not just more swaps at a higher failure bill. The full notebook, the model, and the charts are in the submission. Thank you."

**On-screen text:**  
`Growth is real. Make it healthy.`

---

## Production notes

- **B-roll:** cabinet fans/vents, a rider waiting at a swap station, battery rack close-ups, a support-agent screen (generic).
- **Numbers on screen must match the narration exactly.** Avoid extra claims not in the report.
- **Pacing:** ~150 words per minute. If running long, cut the Gen3 comparison in 0:50–1:10, not the recommendation.
- **Accessibility:** burn in captions; keep contrast high; avoid rapid flashes.
- **Data disclaimer:** include a lower-third: "Synthetic dataset. Figures illustrative."
